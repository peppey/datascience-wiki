# What is Topology?

Topologydates back only a few centuries and examines qualitative, global properties of shapes, so the overall structure and relationships between different parts of a shape. An example of a property reflecting the overall shape structure is the number of holes and the connectivity of the shape.

The observation of connectivity also marked the beginning of topology; in the 18th century, Leonhard Euler introduced a form of the notable Königsberg bridge problem and found that the essential information in the problem was the connectivity of the islands, and not their exact positions.

Exact positions and length measurements do not play a role in topology. Topological shapes are flexible, as if made from rubber. We can bend and stretch them without changing their topological identity. A donut and a coffee mug are distinct geometric objects, but as we can continuously deform them to each other, they are indistinguishable in topology. The same holds for a circle and a square.
