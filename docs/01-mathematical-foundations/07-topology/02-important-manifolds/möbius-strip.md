# Möbius Strip

## TL;DR

A **Möbius strip** is a surface with only **one side** and **one boundary**.

It can be constructed by taking a strip of paper, giving it a half-twist, and joining its two ends.

The Möbius strip is a simple example of a **non-orientable surface**.

## Construction

Start with a rectangular strip:

$$
[0,1]\times[0,1].
$$

Give it a half-twist and identify the two ends.

This identification can be described by

$$
(0,y)\sim(1,1-y).
$$

The reversal of the second coordinate creates the twist.

## Properties

The Möbius strip has:

* **one side**
* **one boundary component**
* **one edge**
* a **non-orientable** surface structure

If you travel along the centerline of a Möbius strip and return to your starting point, your local orientation has been reversed.

## Non-Orientability

On an orientable surface, it is possible to consistently distinguish between two sides, such as "front" and "back".

This is impossible on a Möbius strip.

A path around the strip reverses the orientation of a local coordinate system. Therefore, the Möbius strip is **non-orientable**.

## Boundary

Unlike a cylinder, which has two boundary components, the Möbius strip has only one.

If you draw a line along the middle of the strip and follow it continuously, you eventually return to the starting point after traversing the strip twice.

## Topological Properties

The Möbius strip is a **2-dimensional manifold with boundary**.

It has Euler characteristic

$$
\chi=0.
$$

Its boundary is topologically equivalent to a circle:

$$
\partial M\cong S^1.
$$

## Applications

The Möbius strip appears in:

* topology
* differential geometry
* mathematical visualization
* mechanical belt systems
* art and design

It is particularly useful as an intuitive example of **non-orientability**.
