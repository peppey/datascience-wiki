# Fractals

## TL;DR

A **fractal** is a mathematical object with complex structure that often exhibits **self-similarity** across different scales.

Fractals can be generated by simple recursive rules and can have non-integer dimensions.

---

## Self-Similarity

A key property of many fractals is **self-similarity**.

A smaller part of the object resembles the whole object when magnified.

For example, the **Koch curve** is constructed recursively:

```text
Line
 ↓
Divide into 3 parts
 ↓
Replace the middle part with two sides of a triangle
 ↓
Repeat recursively
```

Each iteration creates more detail at a smaller scale.

---

## Fractal Dimension

Fractals often have a **non-integer dimension**.

For a self-similar object consisting of $N$ copies scaled by a factor $r$, the similarity dimension is:

$$
D =
\frac{\log N}{\log(1/r)}
$$

For example, the **Sierpiński triangle** consists of $3$ copies, each scaled by $1/2$:

$$
D =
\frac{\log 3}{\log 2}
\approx 1.585.
$$

Thus, its dimension lies between $1$ and $2.

## Examples

Common mathematical fractals include:

* **Mandelbrot set**
* **Julia sets**
* **Koch curve**
* **Sierpiński triangle**
* **Cantor set**
* **Barnsley fern**

---

## Iterative Construction

Many fractals are defined through iteration.

For example, the **Mandelbrot set** is based on:

$$
z_{n+1}=z_n^2+c
$$

starting with:

$$
z_0=0.
$$

A complex number $c$ belongs to the Mandelbrot set if the sequence remains bounded.

---

## Fractals in Nature

Approximate fractal structures can be found in many natural phenomena, such as:

* coastlines
* branching trees
* blood vessels
* river networks
* clouds
* snowflakes

Natural objects are generally not perfectly fractal, but they can exhibit fractal-like behavior over certain scales.

---

## Key Idea

Fractals combine **recursion, self-similarity, and complex structure**.

They are studied primarily in **geometry and dynamical systems**, and are also relevant to areas such as computer graphics, signal processing, and the analysis of natural phenomena.
