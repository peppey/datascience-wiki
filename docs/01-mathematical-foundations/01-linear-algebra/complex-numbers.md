# Complex Numbers

## TL;DR

**Complex numbers** extend the real numbers by adding the imaginary unit:

$$
i = \sqrt{-1}
$$

A complex number has the form:

$$
z = a + bi
$$

where:

- `a` is the **real part**
- `b` is the **imaginary part**

---

## Representation

A complex number can be represented as a point in the complex plane:
z = a + bi
Real axis → a
Imaginary axis ↑ b


Example:
z = 3 + 2i

- Real part: `3`
- Imaginary part: `2`

---

## Basic Operations

### Addition

\[
(a+bi)+(c+di)=(a+c)+(b+d)i
\]

### Multiplication

\[
(a+bi)(c+di)=(ac-bd)+(ad+bc)i
\]

---

## Complex Conjugate

The conjugate changes the sign of the imaginary part:
z = a + bi
z* = a - bi


It is used for division and calculating magnitudes.

---

## Magnitude

The magnitude (absolute value) of a complex number is:
|z| = sqrt(a² + b²)

It describes the distance from the origin in the complex plane.

---

## Applications

Complex numbers are important in:

- Fourier transforms
- Signal processing
- Electrical engineering
- Quantum mechanics
- Control systems
