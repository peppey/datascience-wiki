# Kullback–Leibler Divergence

## TL;DR

The **Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence** measures how different one probability distribution is from another.

For discrete probability distributions $P$ and $Q$:

$$
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P|Q)
=

\sum_x P(x)
\log
\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}.
$$

It is widely used in **machine learning, statistics, information theory, and probabilistic modeling**.

## Interpretation

The KL divergence can be interpreted as the additional information required when using $Q$ to represent data generated by $P$.

A smaller value means that $Q$ is closer to $P$.

If

$$
P=Q,
$$

then

$$
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P|Q)=0.
$$

However, KL divergence is **not symmetric**:

$$
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P|Q)
\neq
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(Q|P).
$$

Therefore, it is a **divergence**, not a distance.

## Example

Suppose

$$
P=(0.8,0.2)
$$

and

$$
Q=(0.6,0.4).
$$

Then

$$
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P|Q)
=

0.8\log\frac{0.8}{0.6}
+
0.2\log\frac{0.2}{0.4}.
$$

The result quantifies how much $Q$ differs from $P$.

## Continuous Distributions

For continuous probability distributions, the sum is replaced by an integral:

$$
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P|Q)
=

\int
p(x)
\log
\frac{p(x)}{q(x)}
,dx.
$$

## Important Properties

KL divergence satisfies

$$
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P|Q)\geq 0.
$$

This is known as **Gibbs' inequality**.

However, it does not generally satisfy the properties of a metric:

* it is not symmetric
* it does not satisfy the triangle inequality

Also, if $P(x)>0$ while $Q(x)=0$, then the KL divergence is infinite.

## Cross-Entropy Connection

KL divergence is closely related to **cross-entropy**:

$$
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P|Q)
=

H(P,Q)-H(P),
$$

where $H(P,Q)$ is the cross-entropy and $H(P)$ is the entropy of $P$.

Since $H(P)$ does not depend on $Q$, minimizing cross-entropy with respect to $Q$ is equivalent to minimizing KL divergence.

## Applications

KL divergence is used in:

* classification
* variational inference
* Bayesian inference
* generative models
* distribution matching
* knowledge distillation
* variational autoencoders (VAEs)
